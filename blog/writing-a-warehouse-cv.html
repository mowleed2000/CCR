How to Write a CV for Warehouse Roles

A practical guide to describing warehouse experience clearly, from picking and packing to stock handling and shift work.

Collab Create Recruitment · Practical guide

Warehouse CVs work best when they make your practical experience easy to understand. A hiring manager should quickly see the environments you know, the tasks you have done and the shifts you can work.

Start with a short profile

Use three or four lines to describe your relevant experience and the kind of role you want. For example, mention goods-in, order picking, packing, dispatch or stock replenishment if you have actually done those tasks. If you are new to warehouse work, focus on transferable strengths such as reliability, teamwork, following instructions and working at a steady pace.

Make duties specific

Replace broad phrases such as “warehouse duties” with a short list of real tasks. You might include preparing orders, checking quantities, labelling parcels, keeping work areas tidy or reporting damaged stock. Mention equipment only when you have used it, and describe your level of experience honestly.

Include availability and practical details

State the shift patterns you can consider, your notice period and whether you can reliably reach the workplace. If you have training or certificates relevant to a particular vacancy, list the exact name and status. Do not imply you hold an authorisation that you do not have.

Keep the layout readable

Use clear headings, reverse chronological work history and concise bullet points. Check dates, spelling and contact details before sending your CV. A tailored one-page CV is often easier to scan than a long document full of unrelated information.

Before you apply

Read the vacancy carefully and make sure your CV responds to its actual requirements. If you are unsure how to present your experience, contact Collab Create Recruitment and tell us what roles and shifts you are looking for. Current opportunities depend on vacancies available at the time.
